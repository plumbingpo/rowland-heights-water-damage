Bathroom Water Damage Help in Rowland Heights

A bathroom is designed to handle water on its surfaces, never below them. Every flush and every shower lets a small amount of water by after the caulk cracks, the grout wears down or the seal at the toilet fails. That water gathers where you cannot see it, in the subfloor and inside the tiled wall. We help Rowland Heights homeowners by tracing the leak path, checking its reach with meters, and drying or removing whatever got wet.

These leaks are usually slow, so the damage is often deeper and older than it appears. The tile can look perfectly fine while the subfloor beneath the toilet has gone soft clear through. With thermal imaging and moisture meters, we map what is wet before any demolition, and we open just the sections that will not dry in place.

What It Looks Like

  • Cracked, peeling or mold blackened caulk where the tub or shower meets the wall
  • Dark stains at the base of the toilet, or a bowl that wobbles a little
  • Hours after anyone showered, the grout is still dark with moisture
  • Vinyl flooring that curls at the edges or has discolored near the toilet or tub
  • Musty odor in the bathroom that cleaning does not get rid of
  • A spongy feel in the floor beside the tub or in front of the vanity

Behind the Surface

The Damage You Do Not See

Leaks Inside the Vanity

Behind the towels and bottles kept under the sink, a drain fitting or supply valve can drip for months. Water swells the cabinet bottom, then passes into the back wall and the subfloor.

Trapped Steam

Without a working exhaust fan, the steam from showers has nowhere to go. That moisture settles on walls and ceilings, feeds mold, and slows the drying of anything that is already wet.

Mold Behind Tile

Neither grout nor tile is a waterproof barrier without help. If the board behind them stays damp, mold grows on it, out of reach of any cleaning.

A Failed Shower Pan

With a crack in the liner or pan, water escapes below the shower floor at each use. The surface looks perfect while the framing below stays soaked.

The Clock

How the Damage Progresses

The first few days

Water Gets Past

With every shower, the bad caulk or grout lets a small amount of water through. It soaks into the backer board and the edge of the subfloor, with nothing visible yet.

The first several weeks

Subfloor Stays Wet

Showers come too often for the wood beneath the tile to ever dry. Mold starts on the underside of the floor and inside the wall cavity.

One to three months

Signs Show Up

You see darker grout, loosening tiles and blackened caulk. A stain shows on the ceiling of any room below and slowly grows.

Six months and beyond

Structure Weakens

Around the tub or toilet the subfloor goes soft, and the joists may start rotting. At this point the job means new tile and carpentry on top of the drying.
