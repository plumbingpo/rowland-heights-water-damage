Of all the stages in restoring water damage, drying the structure counts most, and it is the stage most often botched by people without training. Once standing water has been extracted, a great deal of moisture is still held inside the structure: in insulation, concrete, subfloors, wood framing, drywall and other components. This trapped moisture must be scientifically removed through controlled evaporation and dehumidification to prevent the mold growth, wood rot, material degradation and odor problems that develop when structures remain wet.

The structural drying technicians at Rowland Heights Restoration are IICRC-certified and trained in psychrometry, the science of how temperature, humidity and moisture in air relate. For each job they apply it to calculate airflow patterns, temperature settings and the placement of equipment that will give the best conditions for evaporation. Plaster, concrete, hardwood and drywall each dry at their own rate, so each needs its own approach to get down to an acceptable moisture level with no added damage.

Every day of the drying process, we check moisture using thermal imaging cameras, thermo-hygrometers and calibrated moisture meters. We document moisture readings throughout the drying process, creating a detailed drying log that shows progressive moisture reduction in all affected materials. Each day we use those numbers to reposition or retune the equipment so drying stays efficient. A surface that feels dry to your hand proves nothing, so the job is finished only once all affected materials test within the acceptable standards set by IICRC S500.

Structural Drying Prevention Tips

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Moisture can sit deep in a material whose surface feels dry, so rely on the meter readings
  • Keep belongings off the machines and out of the path of the air movers
  • Every hour of run time matters, so resist switching the machines off for some quiet
  • Deal with water intrusion right away, since an early start on drying gives a better result
  • Avoid moving the machines yourself, because their placement follows our moisture map
  • If a new damp spot or smell shows up while drying is underway, let the technician know at once

Why Hire a Professional for Structural Drying?

Drying a structure properly takes science, and guesswork has no place in it. Simply placing fans and hoping for the best is the most common cause of secondary mold damage after water events. It involves daily monitoring, a calculation of the required grain depression (the capacity to remove moisture) and a working knowledge of the way materials absorb moisture and let it go. Without this knowledge, under-drying leaves moisture that feeds mold, while over-drying can damage wood and other materials.

Professional drying equipment is not a bigger version of what stores sell, it is a different kind of machine. Commercial LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers remove 10 to 20 times more moisture from the air than residential dehumidifiers, and high-velocity air movers create the precise airflow patterns needed to accelerate evaporation from structural surfaces. Machines like these cost a great deal and take training to use well, which explains why professional results are so much better than a do-it-yourself effort.

Signs You Need Structural Drying in Rowland Heights

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Blistered paint and lifting wallpaper on a wall that got wet
  • Trapped moisture often announces itself with a persistent musty odor after cleanup
  • Ceilings, floors or walls that still read high on a moisture meter following a leak
  • Wallboard with water stains, puffiness or a spongy feel
  • No visible water left, yet walls or floors still feel cold and damp
  • Baseboards or door trim that swell, warp or pull away from the wall
  • Humidity that will not come down in the affected space, whatever ventilation you try
