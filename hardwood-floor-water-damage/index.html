Hardwood Floor Water Damage Help in Rowland Heights

Wood floors react to water quickly. As the wood absorbs water along its edges and bottom face, it expands, crowds the neighboring boards and cups, leaving the center lower than the sides. That does not mean the floor is lost. Solid hardwood that is dried in the first few days often flattens back out. What matters is removing moisture from the wood and from its subfloor steadily and under control.

Drying mats are how our team handles wet wood floors. Each mat is sealed against the floor and hooked to a vacuum unit, which pulls moisture upward through seams and boards as dehumidifiers dry the air in the room. Expect it to run a week or more, which is longer than drywall or carpet needs. Hold off on the sander until every board is fully dry. If cupped boards are sanded while still wet, crowning shows up down the road.

Warning Signs

  • Boards that feel soft or spongy underfoot or flex more than before
  • Mildew smell coming up through the boards
  • Baseboards or door trim swelling where the floor meets the wall
  • A washboard feel underfoot from edges that stand taller than board centers
  • Board centers humped higher than the edges, called crowning
  • Creaking and popping noises from boards that used to be quiet

The Clock

What Happens if You Wait

First hours

Water Goes Under

Water slips through the seams and spreads between the boards and subfloor. Underneath, the wood remains wet even after the face has been dried.

24 to 48 hours

Edges Begin to Rise

Swelling sets in and board edges lift. You might notice haze in the topcoat. Growth is able to begin below, on raw wood and on the subfloor panels.

Days 3 through 7

Boards Start Lifting

Expanding boards push harder and harder on one another. Edges get crushed together, nails work loose and parts of the floor may start rising from the subfloor.

Past one week

Permanent Damage

Buckling, black staining and mold under the boards become likely. By now replacement, not drying, is the answer for most of the affected section.

Behind the Surface

What Hides Behind It

Drying Too Fast

Heavy airflow or heat dries the face of a board well ahead of its core. Boards may then split, crack or shrink apart for good.

Water That Keeps Moving

Moisture beneath flooring does not stay put. Next door rooms, the area under cabinets and walls, and the ceiling below are all places it can end up.

Sanding Too Early

Leveling a damp, cupped floor with a sander takes wood off the lifted edges. When the wood finishes drying, the centers end up high and the floor is crowned.

Rusting Fasteners

Staples and flooring nails corrode when moisture lingers, and their grip weakens. Dark marks from that rust soak into the boards and can be too deep to sand out.

Our Process

What Happens After You Call

1

Measure the Moisture

Our team meters the wet boards, the subfloor under them and a dry area of floor to compare against. That tells us how deep the water went and whether saving the floor is realistic.

2

Remove Surface Water

Standing water is extracted right away and rugs and furniture are moved off the wet area. The faster water stops feeding the seams, the less the boards swell.

3

Install the Mat System

We tape drying mats to the floor and attach them to a vacuum system, which draws moisture upward through the wood. To avoid cracking, dehumidifiers keep conditions steady and the wood dries at an even rate.

4

Track Progress and Report

Readings are logged every one to two days and the equipment stays until the wet zone equals the dry comparison areas. Then we tell you whether the floor needs refinishing, a few boards replaced, or nothing.
