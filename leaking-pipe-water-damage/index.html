Dealing With Leaking Pipe Water Damage in Rowland Heights

A leaking pipe rarely announces itself. The drip is hidden in a wall cavity, a sink cabinet or beneath the slab, so the first clue tends to be a stain, a spongy patch of floor or an oddly high water bill. Often that means weeks of moisture have already gone into whatever surrounds the pipe. Our team in Rowland Heights finds how far the moisture has spread and dries all of it, not just the part you can see.

Fixing the pipe ends the leak, yet the moisture already inside the wall or floor stays put. Getting it out is the work we do. We use moisture meters and thermal imaging to map the wet materials, open only what has to be opened, and set air movers and dehumidifiers until readings are back to normal. A slow leak gives mold plenty of time to begin, so we inspect for growth too and treat any surface it has reached.

Before We Arrive

  1. Stop the Water SupplyClose the fixture's supply valve if the leak is at a sink or toilet. If you cannot find the source, turn off the main shutoff valve for the house.
  2. Keep Power AwayIf water is near outlets, switches or a light fixture, switch off the breaker for that area. Do not touch wet electrical devices or cords.
  3. Read the MeterWith every tap off, look at the meter. A dial that keeps turning points to a hidden pipe leak, which may be under the slab.
  4. Empty and PhotographEmpty the closet or cabinet by the leak, then photograph the pipe, any stains and any warped material before repairs start or items are discarded.
  5. Get a Moisture CheckAsk for meter readings of the area before anyone patches it closed. Drywall can feel dry to the touch while the cavity behind it is still damp.
Insurance

Does Insurance Cover It?

Insurance treats slow leaks differently from sudden ones. Policies often cover damage that is sudden and accidental. A leak lasting weeks or months usually falls outside that, and certain policies name it as an exclusion. If the leak was concealed and you could not have known, it may be viewed more favorably than a visible leak nobody dealt with. Read your policy and talk to your agent. Our team records where the leak came from, along with photos and moisture readings, to give the adjuster clear facts.

Common Causes

How It Usually Starts

  • A P-trap or drain connection under a sink that loosens and drips each time the basin empties.
  • Shifting soil or abrasion wearing through water lines that run beneath a concrete slab.
  • Supply connections and compression fittings behind toilets or under sinks that have worn out or worked loose.
  • A hairline split left by freezing weather in a line running through a crawl space or outside wall.
  • Water pressure set too high, which wears on every joint and valve until a slow weep begins.
Why Speed Matters

What the Water Damages

Swollen Cabinets

Sink cabinet bottoms are usually particleboard, which swells and crumbles when it soaks up a leak. Swollen board stays swollen, and after a lengthy leak the base often has to be replaced.

Soaked Wall Insulation

Like a sponge, wall insulation takes in water from the pipe and keeps the framing next to it damp. Since a closed wall gives it almost no airflow, soaked insulation is normally removed.

Damaged Flooring

A leak in a wall or under the slab sends water beneath the floor covering. The result is cupped hardwood, laminate with swollen seams, and vinyl or tile coming loose as its adhesive fails.

Mold Inside the Wall

A dark, damp wall cavity is where mold does best. It can spread across the hidden face of the drywall long before anything appears on the room side, which is common when a leak is slow.
