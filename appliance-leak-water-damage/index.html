Appliance Leak Water Damage in Rowland Heights: What to Know

Three appliances cause most of the leaks we see. A washing machine supply hose can split and run at full pressure until someone shuts it off. Dishwashers usually leak a little at a time, at the drain hose or door seal, hidden beneath the cabinets. A refrigerator ice maker line is a thin tube that can drip behind the unit for months. Cleanup is different for each of them.

Our Rowland Heights crew starts by pulling the appliance out and finding where the water went. In kitchens that usually means under the cabinets and beneath the flooring. With a laundry leak we look at the rooms next to it and the wall behind the washer, plus the ceiling below when the laundry sits on an upper floor. Standing water is extracted, absorbed moisture is measured, and drying equipment is sized to fit the wet area.

First Five Minutes

Before We Arrive

  1. Shut the Machine DownStop the cycle and unplug the machine if the outlet and floor around it are dry. If not, switch it off at the breaker instead.
  2. Close the Supply ValveWashers have hot and cold valves on the wall behind them. The dishwasher valve is under the kitchen sink. The small valve for an ice maker line sits behind the refrigerator or underneath.
  3. Soak Up Standing WaterGetting reachable water up with a mop or towels stops it from moving under the cabinets and the floor. Vacuums are safe for this only when built for wet and dry pickup.
  4. Leave the Door ShutIf the washer or dishwasher has not drained, leave it shut. Opening the door mid-cycle can release the whole load of water onto the floor.
  5. Photograph the Failed PartKeep the part that failed, whether a hose, a fitting or a valve, and get photos of it. Your insurer and the appliance maker may both ask to see it.
Insurance

Insurance and Appliance Leak Water Damage

When a machine overflows unexpectedly or a hose bursts, that is typically treated as sudden and accidental, which many homeowners policies cover. What is typically paid is the water damage, while the failed part and the appliance are left out. A claim is harder when an ice maker line or dishwasher seeped slowly, since coverage usually does not extend to gradual leaks. Some insurers also ask about the age and upkeep of the hose. Check your policy, keep the failed part and take photos before cleanup.

Why It Happens

Why This Happens

  • Rubber washing machine supply hoses that weaken with age and split under pressure.
  • The water inlet valve on a washer or dishwasher failing to close, which lets the machine overfill.
  • A washer drain hose that slips out of the standpipe, or a clogged standpipe.
  • Poor installation, such as a dishwasher set out of level or a supply fitting not fully tightened.
  • Water under a refrigerator from a blocked defrost drain or a drain pan with a crack in it.
If It Is Left

What the Water Damages

Hidden Mold at the Back

Behind a refrigerator or a dishwasher it is warm and dark, and nobody checks there often. A slow drip there feeds mold on the wall and floor long before anyone notices.

Cabinet Damage

From below, a leaking dishwasher wets the toe kicks and the side panels of the cabinets. Panels swell and come apart, and a wet cabinet box can keep the wall behind it damp for weeks.

Adjoining Rooms

For hours, a failed washer hose can keep releasing water. That water moves beneath walls into bedrooms, closets and hallways, soaking pad and carpet far from the laundry room.

Ceiling Below an Upstairs Laundry

In a home with an upstairs laundry, leak water passes through the floor and pools on the ceiling underneath. The drywall stains and sags, and the insulation above it stays wet.

Our Process

Our Appliance Leak Water Damage Process

1

Move the Appliance and Inspect

The appliance is disconnected and moved, and the supply is confirmed off. Then thermal imaging and moisture meters show us where water went below flooring, under the cabinets and into the walls nearby.

2

Remove Standing Water

Standing water is extracted from floors and carpet. Where water is trapped under cabinets or floating floors, we remove toe kicks or lift boards to reach it.

3

Save Materials by Drying

Our crew places drying equipment and aims airflow at the hardwood and the cabinets, drying them in place whenever possible to avoid removing them.

4

Sanitize and Take Final Readings

We apply antimicrobial treatment wherever discharge from the washer or the dishwasher made contact. Before the appliance is put back, a last set of readings confirms the floor under it is no longer damp.
