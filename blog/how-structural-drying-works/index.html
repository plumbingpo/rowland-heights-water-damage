How Structural Drying Works in Rowland Heights, CA

Home / Blog / How Structural Drying Works in Rowland Heights, CA

Ask people around Rowland Heights, CA what restoration involves and drying the structure rarely comes up, although it is the core of the work. Once the visible water is gone, a lot is still held in the building itself, in framing lumber, drywall, insulation, subflooring, and concrete. Left in place, that moisture ends in odors, mold, and materials that break down. Below is a plain look at the science professionals rely on, and at why their results are much better than what DIY drying achieves.

The Science of Psychrometry

Psychrometry, a science concerned with gas-vapor mixtures and their thermodynamic and physical properties, is what structural drying is built on. Put simply, it is about the way air movement, relative humidity, and temperature work together to pull water from wet materials. With psychrometric calculations, an IICRC-certified technician works out the setup for a given job: how many dehumidifiers and which type, where the air movers go, and what temperature and humidity to aim for.

Drying Equipment Built for the Job

The equipment used for professional structural drying operates on an entirely different scale than consumer products. An hour of run time takes 15 to 30 or more pints out of the air with one LGR (Low-Grain Refrigerant) dehumidifier, against 2 to 3 pints with a household model. High-velocity air movers create precisely directed airflow at 2,500 CFM or more to speed evaporation from wet surfaces. Under extreme moisture loads or in cold weather, the specialized tool is a desiccant dehumidifier. This equipment represents tens of thousands of dollars in investment and requires specific training to deploy effectively.

The Drying Process Step by Step

Professional structural drying follows a systematic process. First, a thorough moisture assessment maps all affected areas using moisture meters and thermal imaging. From that map come the baseline readings and the plan for where equipment goes. The crew then sets air movers and dehumidifiers where the psychrometric math says they will evaporate and capture the most moisture. Temperature may be raised to increase evaporation rates. A technician returns daily to take moisture readings at all monitoring points, adjust equipment as needed, and document progress. The job is not finished until every wet material tests at an acceptable level against a dry reference standard.

What to Expect for Drying Time

Expect 3 to 5 days for the majority of residential jobs when proper commercial gear is used. The timeline depends on several factors: the volume of moisture present, the types of materials affected (concrete dries much slower than drywall), ambient conditions, building construction, and the number and type of equipment deployed. Wood framing typically dries in 3 to 4 days, standard drywall in 2 to 3 days, and concrete in 5 to 7 days or more. Your technician will provide a projected timeline based on initial moisture readings and adjust it as drying progresses.

Why Daily Readings Matter

Without a daily check, drying is only a guess. Only calibrated readings can tell you if a material is drying at all, if it is on pace, and if it has reached a safe moisture level. Deep in a material, moisture can remain at a dangerous level even though the surface feels dry when touched. Only calibrated moisture meters can provide this verification. Professional drying documentation also gives your insurance company proof that proper industry protocols were followed, supporting your claim and protecting your property value.
