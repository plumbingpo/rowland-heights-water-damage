Wet Carpet Drying: What Rowland Heights Homeowners Should Know

A wet carpet is hiding a lot more water than you can see. Often the pad is full of water and the subfloor is wet beneath it, even when the fibers you touch feel only slightly damp. A shop vacuum and a stack of towels only deal with the surface. In Rowland Heights, we pull the water out, take moisture meter readings on each layer, and place drying equipment that works down to the subfloor.

Two things decide if a carpet is worth saving: the cleanliness of the water and the time it has sat wet. When a clean supply line is the source and we arrive early, drying the carpet where it lies usually works. Gray water from an appliance often means the pad goes and the carpet gets cleaned and treated. After sewage or flood water from outdoors, neither the carpet nor the pad stays.

What It Looks Like

  • Carpet that squishes underfoot or leaves your socks damp when you walk on it
  • Wet tack strip showing through as dark, rusty streaks next to the baseboard
  • The room smells sour and stale, especially after it has been closed for a while
  • As the carpet dries, yellowish or brown stains wick to the top of the pile
  • Dampness that spreads outward on its own, well after the leak was stopped
  • Paint blistering near the floor, or baseboard trim that has puffed up
What We Do

What Happens After You Call

1

Find the Edges

Thermal imaging and meter readings show us where the water went, closets and the space under walls included. We also identify the water category, because that decides what can stay.

2

Pull Out the Water

With weighted extractors, carpet and the pad beneath it are pressed and emptied in one pass. This removes far more than a wand alone, and it shortens the drying time that follows.

3

Dry All the Layers

The carpet is either pulled back so the pad can go, or left down with air blown beneath it, whichever the job needs. Dehumidifiers and air movers stay on until the subfloor and the carpet each test dry.

4

Clean and Reset

We treat the carpet and subfloor with an antimicrobial where needed, lay new pad if the old one came out, restretch the carpet onto the tack strip, and clean it.

Why Speed Matters

How the Damage Progresses

First hours

Water Spreads Sideways

Under the surface, the pad pulls water outward, well beyond what looks wet. It slips beneath walls into neighboring rooms while the tack strip begins to absorb it.

24 to 48 hours

Smell and Mold Start

The damp pad breeds bacteria, and the sour smell of wet carpet follows. Mold can start to grow on dust caught deep in the pile and on the carpet back.

Days 3 through 7

The Carpet Breaks Down

The latex that holds the backing together breaks down, so the carpet delaminates and ripples. Nails in the tack strip start rusting, and the subfloor swells.

After a week

Past Saving

By now the carpet and pad usually have to come out. Often mold has spread to the subfloor and the base of the drywall, which adds to the job.

Hidden Risks

The Damage You Do Not See

Mold on the Backing

Mold tends to start underneath, on the carpet back, because that side stays damp and dark. You smell it long before you ever see it on top.

Furniture Marks

Wood stain and metal feet on furniture bleed into wet carpet. The furniture also draws up water and swells, and the marks it leaves often never come out.

Water Quality Drops

A clean water spill does not remain clean for long. As it sits in carpet and pad it picks up soil and bacteria, so a carpet that could have been dried may have to be removed.

Wet Wall Bottoms

Under the carpet, water travels until it hits the bottom of the walls. Because carpet covers the edge, no one sees the drywall and baseboards drawing the water upward.
